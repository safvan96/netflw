How Electromagnetic Meters Work
🔵
Faraday's Law
Magnetic field applied across pipe
Conductive liquid moving through field
generates a voltage proportional to velocity
→ No obstruction. No pressure drop. No wear.

Works with any conductive liquid (conductivity >5 µS/cm)

Technical Transparency

An honest note about your thinner line

⚠️

Solvents & Thinners Require Special Consideration

Electromagnetic flow meters work on Faraday's Law — they require the liquid to be electrically conductive (minimum ~5 µS/cm). Most solvents and thinners have very low conductivity, which means a standard electromagnetic meter may not work for your thinner production line.

🔵

Lines 1 & 3 — Confirmed Compatible

Emulsion paints (water-based) and resin systems typically have conductivity well above 5 µS/cm. Electromagnetic meters are ideal for these applications.

📡

Line 2 (Thinners) — Ultrasonic Recommended

For your thinner/solvent line, we recommend an ultrasonic clamp-on meter — no pipe cutting, no chemical contact, works with any liquid regardless of conductivity.

Why we're telling you this: We sent you a technical questionnaire to confirm exact conductivity values and pipe specifications. Our goal is to recommend the right product — not just sell you something. This is how we build long-term partnerships.
